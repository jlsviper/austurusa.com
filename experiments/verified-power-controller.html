Formally Verified Power-Converter Controller

EXP-005 · Proposed research and engineering project · Digital PWM / safety kernel / power electronics · 12 October 2026

Project status — concept / architecture proposal. No RTL implementation, formal proof, FPGA prototype, fabricated ASIC, board demonstration, or certification evidence has been completed or published for EXP-005 as of this revision. Every guarantee below is a proposed verification target, not a result.
Research premise. Can a small, independent digital safety kernel enforce a precisely specified switching-command envelope around an arbitrarily complex or even misbehaving PWM controller, with machine-checked proofs of the kernel's invariants and separately measured assumptions covering real gate drivers and power hardware?

1. Partition: flexible control, independent protection

The controller may implement conventional voltage/current-mode control, programmed PWM timing, adaptive compensation, or experimental algorithms. Its outputs are requests, not final gate commands. A separate, constrained safety kernel mediates those requests and exclusively owns the registered gate-command outputs. Configuration of the flexible controller cannot bypass the kernel.

The partition borrows from runtime-assurance / Simplex ideas: isolate the trusted safety path rather than claim formal correctness of an unrestricted controller. This architectural pattern is established prior art; an open, reproducible chain from kernel-level proofs through physical implementation and measured switching behavior would be the project's intended contribution, not a demonstrated novelty claim.

2. Candidate formal contract

Proof statements will quantify any legal controller request sequence under explicit reset, clock, observation, and environmental assumptions. Parameters D, N, R and M are not fixed or proven in this proposal.

NonoverlapFor a complementary half-bridge output pair, HS and LS commands are never simultaneously asserted. Each commanded polarity change includes at least D registered-clock intervals of both-off dead time. Gate-pin and FET-level timing are separate measured claims.
Current-limit reactionOnce the kernel has sampled a qualified overcurrent indication, the corresponding gate command deasserts within a bounded number of kernel cycles. A bound from the raw asynchronous comparator pin additionally depends on synchronization / metastability assumptions; fast hardware short-circuit protection may need a separate asynchronous driver shutdown.
Pulse boundsMaximum on-time, permitted duty range, minimum off-time, and optional demagnetization interlocks cannot be overridden by controller commands. For a flyback, safe core reset requires topology-specific volt-second and demagnetization analysis; off-time alone is not proof of reset.
Fault containmentObserved faults transition the command path to an all-off / latched-fault state within the stated digital bound. Re-enabling passes through explicit qualification and soft-start; no direct fault-to-full-duty transition.
Safe configurationAll register values, including extreme and illegal values, are either clamped or rejected so the kernel's hard safety minima and maxima remain invariant.
WatchdogA windowed or challenge-response protocol detects specified host/controller heartbeat failures. A heartbeat toggled by arbitrary broken software is not treated as sufficient evidence of correct control.
Conditional recoveryIf faults stay clear and the required reset / rearm conditions hold for R cycles, the kernel reaches soft-start within M cycles. This is bounded conditional progress, not an unconditional guarantee that a faulty plant recovers.

3. The physical assumptions matter as much as the proofs

  1. Clock failure. A digital watchdog driven by the same stopped clock cannot shut off a stuck-high command. An independent timebase, driver maximum-on-time, clock-loss supervisor, or equivalent out-of-band shutdown must be justified and tested.
  2. Registered gates. Route PWM commands directly from designated output flip-flops, with no combinational logic after the final registering stage; check the synthesized/netlist structure as well as RTL properties. Downstream pads and drivers still require timing characterization.
  3. Propagation and switching skew. Output-pin dead time is not MOSFET dead time. Include driver turn-on/turn-off delay spread, isolator timing where applicable, FET charge / Miller behavior, and operating extremes in a minimum remaining dead-time budget.
  4. Power-up, reset and selection. Ensure pull-downs and an independent driver enable keep the bridge inhibited until power-good, reset release, safe configuration and project selection are valid. A shared Tiny Tapeout I/O mux adds its own ownership boundary.
  5. Asynchronous fault inputs. A two-flop synchronizer provides a practical CDC scheme, but no finite unconditional bound exists from a raw async edge without a metastability/environment assumption. Report latency from the synchronized sample separately from expected raw-pin-to-gate latency, including comparator and driver delays.
  6. Analog plant behavior. Converter stability, transformer saturation, current-sense accuracy, comparator behavior, MOSFET failure modes, PCB parasitics, startup transients, and thermal limits are not established by a digital RTL proof.
  7. Fault injection. Verify fault persistence, priority ordering, stuck requests, invalid register sequences, reset interruption, heartbeat timing errors, and simultaneous asynchronous events. Cover properties are needed to show the design is not trivially always-off.

4. Prototype and evidence plan

Phase 0Requirements, hazard analysis, architectural assumptions, clock/reset contract, input qualification, state diagram, preliminary analog timing budget.
Phase 1Implement synthesizable kernel and an intentionally adversarial programmable PWM requester; write properties and run bounded/unbounded formal checks with SymbiYosys. Publish reproducible scripts, assertions, witnesses, and counterexamples.
Phase 2FPGA bench with a current-limited, low-voltage 12 V input / 5 V output synchronous-buck demonstration; verify driver dead time, fault handling, startup, and clock-loss behavior at the physical gate pins.
Phase 3Optional digital-only Tiny Tapeout implementation with comparator/fault inputs and registered gate-command outputs; complete physical checks for the exact taped-out revision. The external driver and independent inhibit are off-chip.
Phase 4Repeat across voltage, temperature and representative boards; publish timing uncertainty, observed failure limits and fault-injection results. FIPE may help characterize digital edge timing and interface timing, subject to its calibrated resolution.

Evidence goal: a requirements-to-assertion-to-test traceability matrix, source-control revisions, tool and proof logs, synthesized output-path checks, physical measurements, and an explicit table of assumptions and unresolved hazards. This can be useful in high-assurance engineering; it is not a safety certification or a certification-ready product.

5. What is not claimed

No formal results have yet been produced for this project. Proving the sampled digital command envelope does not prove continuous-time loop stability, physical absence of shoot-through, electrical shock safety, system reliability, or regulatory compliance. Claims such as silicon novelty, medical-device suitability, ISO 26262 compliance, and fabricated demonstration require future independent evidence.

6. Prior art and tools

  • CMU SEI: An Architectural Description of the Simplex Architecture (1996) — established runtime-assurance partitioning.
  • Mehmood et al., Black-Box Simplex (2022) — runtime assurance with complex controllers.
  • YosysHQ SymbiYosys — planned formal verification front end and proof workflow.
