AP-002: Adaptive Causal Worlds

Austurusa · problem statement v0.1 · 18 September 2026 · status: open, active

Problem. Can a causal inference system represent uncertainty over the family of causal assumptions that generated a new dataset, recognize when none of its familiar causal families is adequate, and use that uncertainty to decide when a causal estimate should be trusted, withheld, or tested by intervention?
Artifacts: problem statement [PDF] · pdfLaTeX source [.tex]

1. Intuition

Modern causal foundation models can be trained across large families of simulated structural causal models and then estimate causal effects on new datasets without retraining. The resulting model inherits assumptions from the synthetic causal prior used during pretraining.

AP-002 asks what happens when reality does not belong cleanly to that prior. Rather than forcing one model of the world, the system should ask which causal families remain compatible with the evidence.

$$\text{observations}\rightarrow\text{belief over plausible causal worlds}\rightarrow\text{causal conclusion, uncertainty, or abstention}.$$

2. Worlds and universes

A causal world is one structural causal model, written schematically as

$$\mathcal{M}=(G,f,P_{\varepsilon}),$$

where $G$ is a causal graph, $f$ contains the structural mechanisms, and $P_{\varepsilon}$ describes exogenous noise.

A causal universe in this problem is a family or prior distribution over possible worlds:

$$\mathcal{M}\sim\Pi_k.$$

Different families may favor linear or nonlinear mechanisms, different noise structures, hidden confounding, or nonstationarity. The research target is uncertainty not only within a chosen family, but over the suitability of the family itself:

$$P(\Pi_k,G,\theta\mid D).$$

3. The important limitation

Observational data cannot, in general, reveal a unique true causal world. Distinct causal models can generate the same observational distribution. If two worlds are observationally indistinguishable, a correct system must preserve that ambiguity rather than manufacture confidence.

This changes the goal from “identify the true universe” to estimate compatibility over causal world families. When passive evidence is insufficient, the next question is whether an intervention could distinguish the remaining alternatives.

4. Working hypothesis

Assume frozen causal estimators $F_1,\ldots,F_K$, each associated with a different causal prior $\Pi_k$. Given a new observational dataset $D$, estimate

$$q_k(D)\approx P(\Pi_k\text{ is adequate for the relevant causal query}\mid D).$$

The system can then route to, average over, or abstain from causal experts according to that compatibility. An explicit unsupported state is necessary when none of the available families explains the evidence well enough.

5. First falsifiable experiments

  1. Reproduce released causal-foundation-model behavior locally.
  2. Test whether frozen models trained under different causal priors are genuinely complementary across controlled data-generating families.
  3. Hide the simulator family label and test whether observational diagnostics can predict which expert is safer.
  4. Evaluate only after freezing held-out mechanism families and negative controls.
  5. Reject the approach if adaptive selection cannot beat a fixed expert or fixed ensemble on held-out causal risk.

6. Longer-term question

If several causal families remain plausible and they predict different outcomes under an intervention, the system can ask which experiment would be maximally informative:

$$a^*=\arg\max_a I(K;Y_a\mid D,\operatorname{do}(a)).$$

The long-term loop is therefore observation, uncertainty over possible worlds, experiment selection, falsification, and belief update. AP-002 does not present this as an AGI claim; it studies a narrow, testable component of scientific reasoning.

7. Status

No novelty claim is made on this page. The immediate work is reproduction, prior-art tracking, and controlled falsification. The PDF contains the fuller problem statement, current prior-art boundary, kill criteria, and experimental program.
